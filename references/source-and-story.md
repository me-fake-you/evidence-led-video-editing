# Source-grounded story

## Selection and evidence

Reuse an existing authorized index before rescanning. For a large approved batch, inspect metadata first, then low-cost semantic proxies, full-frame-rate candidate windows, and original-quality final selects. Sparse frames cannot establish smooth motion, complete events or sound quality. A photo-only job does not automatically need video ASR or silence removal.

Give selected assets stable identifiers and content hashes; retain source/timeline ranges and time bases. Preserve timestamp/time-zone uncertainty. Record four distinct fields when relevant: observed content, inference, verified claim with supporting evidence, and editorial suggestion. A filename, model score or general species article alone does not prove the specific scene's location or behavioral cause.

Track identity, location, chronology and event continuity independently. A larger subject may result from zoom or crop rather than physical approach. A thematic montage is valid; do not use narration, matched actions or sound to imply unsupported factual continuity.

Reject unusable material before attempting heroic repair. Similar-looking shots may contain distinct actions; different files may repeat one event. Check source overlap, visual similarity, event identity and narrative redundancy separately. Record reasons for intentional repetition or unusual quality tradeoffs, not a universal zero-repeat score.

## Build the story from coverage

State the intended viewer and the value the footage can actually provide. Use a structure suited to the material: observation and discovery, verified route choices, place/time contrasts, or sensory experience. Do not invent adversity, personal emotions, species, prices or travel logistics to complete a template.

For each meaningful section, identify candidate sources, new information/experience, sound role and any missing coverage. Count visual timeline coverage without adding overlapping sound duration or counting simultaneous collage images as extra seconds. Still-image duration is an editorial decision, not an intrinsic fixed capacity. Keep requested length when supported; explain a genuine content shortage instead of looping, arbitrarily slowing or adding empty narration.

## Write for the viewer

Separate supported user experience, verified public information and editorial observation. Director notes such as “establish space” are not audience narration. First-person scanning is a review aid, not a ban on neutral phrases such as “let's look.” Avoid speaking over a scene merely to name what is obvious; do not reduce all writing to sterile labels either.

When practical information is missing, use a supported observation-led concept or identify the gap; do not pretend an aesthetic montage is a route guide. Independent language versions should share facts but use natural phrasing and actual speech timing. Do not claim native-human review unless performed.

If evidence changes, identify affected titles, narration, captions and platform copy for re-review. Call propagation automatic only when the dependency behavior has been implemented and tested. A list of empty evidence IDs is not evidence validation.
